Meta-Analysis: When and How

From a pile of studies to one honest answer · Department of Community Medicine, AFMC Pune

A teaching session for the YLNC on when to pool studies and how to do it well. One running example is used throughout: 13 randomised trials of BCG vaccine against tuberculosis (Colditz et al. 1994).

Open the presentation

What the slides cover

  1. Why meta-analysis? What it is, why combine studies, and when not to.
  2. The workflow. From question to pooled estimate, data abstraction, tidy data, and risk of bias before pooling.
  3. Effect measures. Choosing a measure and computing it from a 2 × 2 table.
  4. Pooling. Inverse-variance weighting, fixed vs random effects, and estimating τ².
  5. Heterogeneity. Q, I² and the prediction interval.
  6. Reading a forest plot. The BCG forest plot, part by part.
  7. Explaining heterogeneity. Subgroups, meta-regression on latitude, and leave-one-out.
  8. Small-study effects. Funnel plots, Egger's test and trim-and-fill.
  9. Certainty of evidence. Where meta-analysis meets GRADE.
  10. Doing it in R. A complete analysis in a few lines, software options, common pitfalls, take-home messages and resources.
